How it works

Three stages, about thirty seconds.

Nothing is pre-computed and no list is bought in. Every scan generates its candidates, checks them live, and measures each one against your real site as it stands today.

What it catches

A score ranks. A class tells you what to do about it.

A cloned login page, a mail-capable domain with no website, and a parked registration are three different problems. So nakli names them.

Homograph attack

Visually indistinguishable from your domain. No user can catch this by reading it.

Credential harvester

Imitates your site and asks for a password. Built to take logins.

Active clone

Serving a copy of your page right now.

Email spoofer

Can receive and send mail as your brand, with no website to give it away.

Redirector

Bounces visitors elsewhere — often traffic resale or a staging step.

Brand squat

Live site trading on your name.

Parked

Registered and held, not yet weaponised. Worth watching.

Dormant

Resolves but serves nothing. No immediate threat.

Features

Built to be acted on, not just admired.

Every finding carries its evidence, its reasoning and its raw data, because a list of scary domains nobody can verify is worse than no list at all.

~1,800 candidates per brand

Typos, homoglyphs, combosquats and TLD swaps, emitted in priority tiers so brand.net is reached before the long tail — never sorted alphabetically.

DNS, HTTP, MX and favicon

Each candidate is resolved, then raced over both schemes, with mail records and favicon hashes compared against a live baseline of your real site.

Scores that state their reasons

Scoring is additive and every point carries a reason in plain language — favicon identical to brand, password field present.

Eight threat classifications

The first matching pattern wins, most dangerous first, so a credential harvester is never filed next to a parked domain.

Unicode homograph reveal

Punycode decoding and confusable-skeleton comparison expose the characters no human catches by reading — then show you the exact codepoint.

Evidence archived at scan time

High and medium findings keep their captured page in object storage, so the proof outlives the takedown that removes it.

Live progress, not a spinner

Server-sent events stream every lookup as it lands — one cell per candidate, filling in as the answers arrive.

CSV and JSON export

Fourteen columns per finding: score, band, threat, IPs, MX, similarity percentages, punycode and the full reason list.

Every scan is replayable

Results persist with their grid and derived fields, so a shared link reopens the exact scan you saw — then re-runs it live on demand.

Someone has already registered a version of your name.

The only question is which one, and what it is currently serving. Find out in about thirty seconds.

nakli. Hindi for counterfeit.

Read-only checks against publicly served pages, rate limited. Findings are candidates for human review, not verdicts — a domain scoring 97 may be your own defensive registration. Counts are a floor, never a total: when a target throttles us we under-report rather than over-report.
